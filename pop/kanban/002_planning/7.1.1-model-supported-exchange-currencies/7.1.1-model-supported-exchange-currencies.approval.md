Two blocking contradictions remain:

1. **AC2 contradicts the plan's own risk fallback and the closed migration language.** AC2 requires "PostgreSQL enforces ... at most one ACTIVE mapping per code" and its pass condition asserts a second active mapping per code fails under `pnpm db:test`. The closed `migration.safe.json` vocabulary cannot express that: `createIndex` in `pop/scripts/migration-policy.mjs` emits only plain column lists (no partial/WHERE or expression index), and row-local CHECK constraints cannot enforce a cross-row invariant. Yet the Risks section simultaneously authorizes "application-only enforcement with the serializing transaction" as an acceptable fallback. AC2, the F01 delivery text ("database-enforced single-active-per-code"), and the fallback are mutually exclusive. The replan must commit to one mechanism — note that a single-active pointer table (e.g. `code UNIQUE` + `pair_id UNIQUE NOT NULL` FK `ON DELETE RESTRICT`) **is** expressible in the safe language via `createTable` + `foreignKey` — and align AC2, the risk, and the abort conditions with that choice.

2. **The immutable-history registration model collides with an existing constraint the plan never mentions.** `CatalogCurrencyPair` already carries `@@unique([currencyUuid, exchangeCurrencyUuid], map: "catalog_currency_pair_uuids_key")` (`prisma/schema.prisma:148`). The plan's "registration creates a new row; historical rows are never deleted or remapped" makes re-registering an identical UUID pair (restore after mistaken deactivation, or replace then replace-back) violate that unique constraint, and the plan's own abort conditions forbid destructive constraint replacement — leaving no sanctioned path. The replan must define explicit semantics against this constraint: either typed rejection of re-registering a historically seen pair (with register requiring an existence lookup), a defined restore/reactivate path, or a sanctioned non-destructive constraint evolution.
